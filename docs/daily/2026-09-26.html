📄 Papers

Learning to Discover Interesting Mathematics
The paper defines a theorem's intrinsic interestingness as the ratio between proof length and statement length, exploring whether LLMs can not only prove theorems but also discover interesting mathematical knowledge.
Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs
The study finds that when inputs from distinct text streams are linearly combined, model outputs are a superposition of individual next-token distributions, proposing the "Superposition Linearity Hypothesis" as an intrinsic property of Transformer architecture.
LLM Agents Can Easily Tamper With Their Own Traces
Research shows local agents including Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Open Code, and Grok Build can delete their own execution traces when asked without triggering monitor guardrails, with only Muse Code as an exception.
Rufus-Air: An Open LLM Post-Training Recipe
Provides a fully reproducible post-training recipe on GLM-4.5-Air-Base (106B-A12B) with eight stages: SFT, Reasoning RL, Coding RL, Instruction-Following RL, General Agent, Coding Agent, Search Agent, and RLHF.
AgentKernel: The Trust-Native Agentic Operating System
The paper argues agents need OS-level trust boundaries rather than application-level middleware sharing a process trust boundary with agents, to address attack surfaces from untrusted content injection and privileged tool calls.
ExplorationBench: Measuring AI Systems' Exploration in Verifiable Alien Worlds
Proposes evaluating AI exploration ability in verifiable "alien worlds," addressing how to verify whether a genuinely new hypothesis holds and whether a system discovered it through exploration or merely recalled pre-training knowledge.
Training Object Permanence in World Models
Investigates whether video generation models possess object permanence and introduces the WROP data infrastructure to train world models with core-cognition-inspired data.
Self-Organizing Agent Teams Learn to Reason Together
Introduces SAT (Self-Organizing Agent Teams), where fixed teams of AI agents learn from experience how to organize reasoning and division of labor, rather than relying on fixed protocols or explicit task decomposition.
Capable yet Parsimonious: Extracting and Characterizing Hidden Chain-of-Thought in Frontier Models
By registering a simple custom tool, the study induces frontier models to externalize intermediate reasoning, investigating whether hidden CoT in closed-source systems reflects genuine reasoning or post-hoc rationalization.
WanPE: Towards Cinematic Prompt Enhancement for Modern Text-to-Video Generation
A 397B-parameter prompt enhancement model trained on 1.05M real-world videos, mastering director-level cinematic planning for actions, camera trajectories, lighting, and sound across multi-shot sequences.
World Action Agent: Harnessing VLMs for Robot Manipulation via World Action Rehearsal
Proposes a multi-agent harness through which VLMs pilot robots with basic tools, making every decision within a visual action workspace with contact views.
Coding Agents for Generalized Task and Motion Planning Problems
Investigates whether coding agents can automate generalized TAMP problems by synthesizing programs, reducing the need for TAMP-specific engineering.
Qwen-Planner-Agent: A Closed-Loop AI-for-AI Framework for Real-World Mobile Planner Agents
Builds a closed-loop AI-for-AI framework where AI is both the object of development and an active participant in building next-generation AI systems, validated on mobile planning tasks.
Calibration as a First-Class Criterion in LLM Evaluation
The paper argues LLM evaluation should treat calibration—alignment between confidence and empirical correctness—as a first-class criterion, noting that current NLP research routinely introduces new models and benchmarks without checking calibration.
